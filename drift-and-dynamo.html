02 — Where it comes from

Three sources,
no catalogue.

Nothing is bought new except the wiring and the oil. Everything else is found, and what it was stays visible.

Driftwood

Gathered by hand along a 3 km stretch of coast, in the week after winter storms. I take only wood that is already dead and loose — never cut, never dragged off a living dune.

  • Sea-oak, poplar, pine · rarely walnut
  • Rinsed of salt, air-dried 6–9 months
  • No kiln, no stain, no filler
  • Cracks left open, not resin-poured

Repurposed steel

Pulled from scrapyards and farm sheds. Degreased, wire-brushed by hand, then clear-lacquered with the pitting left exactly as found — that surface took forty years to make.

  • Dacia 1300 pistons, bezels, rocker arms
  • IFA W50 pulleys · tractor gearbox spindles
  • Brass unions, copper brake line
  • Never sandblasted, never chromed

Oddities

The drawer everything else lives in. Some of it waits two years for the right piece of wood to turn up, and some of it never gets used at all.

  • Porcelain fuse holders, 1960s substation
  • Bakelite toggle switches
  • Brass sextant vernier plates
  • Telegraph insulators, lab glass

05 — How a lamp happens

Nine months of waiting,
two weeks of work.

The order matters. Skip the drying and the wood splits around the socket a year later, in someone's living room.

01

Gathering

Walked after winter storms, at low water. Only what is already dead and loose. Most of what I pick up goes back.

Nov – Mar · a few hours a week
02

Curing

Fresh-water rinse to pull the salt, then stacked in an open shed with airflow on all four sides. No kiln — kiln-dried driftwood checks and grey-fades.

6 – 9 months
03

Salvage

Scrapyard runs. Degrease in a soda bath, wire-brush by hand, stop before the pitting goes. Anything I have to grind smooth was the wrong part.

1 day
04

Joinery

The wood decides where the socket goes, not the drawing. Hand-cut mortise, brass hardware, no visible screws, no glue holding weight.

2 – 5 days
05

Wiring

E27 ceramic socket, braided cotton flex, inline switch, strain relief at both ends. Every lamp is continuity- and insulation-tested before it leaves.

Half a day · CE tested
06

Finish

Three coats of hardwax oil, 24 hours between, cut back with 400 grit. No stain, ever — the colour is ten years of seawater.

4 days

06 — The workshop

One bench,
one pair of hands.

I started this because I couldn't throw away a piece of oak that the sea had spent a decade sanding better than I ever could. The car parts came later, out of stubbornness: a piston is a beautifully machined object that stops being useful the moment one ring goes, and there are fields full of them.

I don't make series. Two lamps from the same tree will still be two different lamps, and if you ask me for a matching pair I'll tell you honestly which one to take. What you get is a signed piece, a written note of where every part of it came from, and someone who will rewire it for you in ten years.

07 — Commissions

Bring me the object.

A grandfather's tool, a part off the car you sold, a piece of wood you carried home from a holiday. It works best when the material already means something.

01

Send a photo

The object, or the corner of the room it has to light. Ceiling height and a rough sense of how dark you want it is enough to start.

Reply within 2 days
02

Sketch and price

A hand drawing, the materials I'd use, and a fixed price. No fee for this, and no obligation. Half up front if you go ahead.

Within a week
03

On the bench

Photos as it goes. Delivered crated, with the bulb in it and a card listing where each part came from.

3 – 5 weeks
Please note

I take about one commission a month, and I turn down anything that needs the wood to be stained, straightened or matched to a sample. If that's what the room needs, I'll happily point you to someone who does it well.
